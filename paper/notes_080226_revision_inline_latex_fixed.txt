REPLACE: POPULATION MODEL INTERPRETATION

POPULATION MODEL

Assume that the true historical slopes come from a common population:

$$\beta_s\sim\mathcal{N}\left(\mu_\beta,\tau_\beta^2\right).$$

The parameter $$\mu_\beta$$ is the average underlying cross-sectional return payoff associated with a one-standard-deviation increase in the SEC signal.

The parameter $$\tau_\beta^2$$ is the variance across dates in the true cross-sectional return payoff of the signal.

A small $$\tau_\beta^2$$ means that the latent slope is reasonably similar across historical rebalance dates.

A large $$\tau_\beta^2$$ means that the latent payoff differs substantially across dates.

This is different from the sampling variance $$v_s$$.

The sampling variance $$v_s$$ measures uncertainty in the direct estimate $$\widehat{\beta}_s$$ for one historical date.

The between-date variance $$\tau_\beta^2$$ measures genuine date-to-date variation in the latent slopes after accounting for estimated slope noise.

The baseline method-of-moments estimate is:

$$\widehat{\tau}_{\beta,t}^2=\max\left\{0,\operatorname{Var}_{s\in W_h(t)}\left(\widehat{\beta}_s\right)-\frac{1}{|W_h(t)|}\sum_{s\in W_h(t)}v_s\right\}.$$

The interpretation is:

Observed dispersion of the direct historical slopes = genuine dispersion of the latent historical slopes + average slope-estimation noise.

The lower bound is imposed because a variance cannot be negative.

With overlapping forward-return windows, adjacent direct slopes may be dependent. The method-of-moments estimate remains the primary transparent estimator, but its uncertainty must be evaluated using dependence-preserving resampling and non-overlapping-date sensitivity analyses.

Do not replace the sample variance in this equation with a Newey-West long-run variance. A Newey-West variance estimates uncertainty in a time-series mean; it is not a direct estimator of latent cross-date heterogeneity.

HISTORICAL SHRINKAGE AND CURRENT POINT-IN-TIME FORECAST

PRECISION WEIGHTS AND POPULATION MEAN

Define:

$$a_{s,t}=\frac{1}{\widehat{\tau}_{\beta,t}^2+v_s}.$$

A historical slope with a smaller total variance receives greater weight. A noisier historical slope receives less weight.

The empirical-Bayes estimate of the population mean slope is:

$$\widehat{\mu}_{\beta,t}=\frac{\sum_{s\in W_h(t)}a_{s,t}\widehat{\beta}_s}{\sum_{s\in W_h(t)}a_{s,t}}.$$

Only completed historical dates in $$W_h(t)$$ are used.

SHRUNK HISTORICAL SLOPES

For a completed historical date $$s$$, define:

$$B_{s,t}=\frac{\widehat{\tau}_{\beta,t}^2}{\widehat{\tau}_{\beta,t}^2+v_s}.$$

The empirical-Bayes estimate of the latent historical slope is:

$$\widehat{\beta}_{s\mid t}^{EB}=B_{s,t}\widehat{\beta}_s+(1-B_{s,t})\widehat{\mu}_{\beta,t}.$$

The quantity $$\widehat{\beta}_{s\mid t}^{EB}$$ estimates the latent slope belonging to historical date $$s$$ using information available through later date $$t$$.

If $$v_s$$ is large, $$B_{s,t}$$ is small and the noisy direct estimate is pulled strongly toward the population mean.

If $$v_s$$ is small, $$B_{s,t}$$ is larger and the direct estimate receives more weight.

These are substantive empirical-Bayes estimates of completed historical effects. They are retrospective because the direct slope $$\widehat{\beta}_s$$ requires the realized forward return beginning at $$s$$. They were not forecasts available at the beginning of date $$s$$.

CURRENT POINT-IN-TIME FORECAST

At current forecast date $$t$$, the forward return beginning at $$t$$ has not yet been observed. Therefore, the direct current slope $$\widehat{\beta}_t$$ is unavailable.

Under the exchangeable random-effects model, the predicted slope for the new period is the estimated population mean:

$$\widehat{\mu}_{\beta,t}.$$

No additional symbol is required for the current forecast coefficient.

The unrestricted stock-level expected active return is:

$$\widehat{\mu}_{i,t}=\widehat{\mu}_{\beta,t}z_{i,t}.$$

Because $$z_{i,t}$$ is centered within the forecast universe:

$$\sum_{i\in U_t}\widehat{\mu}_{i,t}=0.$$

These are expected active returns relative to the equal-weight research universe. They are not forecasts of total stock returns.

OPTIONAL SIGN-CONSTRAINED FORECAST

Define:

$$\widehat{\mu}_{\beta,t}^{+}=\max\left\{0,\widehat{\mu}_{\beta,t}\right\}.$$

The sign-constrained stock-level forecast is:

$$\widehat{\mu}_{i,t}^{+}=\widehat{\mu}_{\beta,t}^{+}z_{i,t}.$$

The zero floor is not part of empirical Bayes. It is an additional economic portfolio rule.

The unrestricted forecast must remain the primary statistical estimate and must always be stored and reported.

The sign-constrained version is a prespecified portfolio sensitivity that turns the factor off when the estimated average payoff is nonpositive. It does not reverse the factor.

The distinction is:

$$\widehat{\beta}_{s\mid t}^{EB}=\text{retrospective shrunk estimate of the latent slope for completed historical date }s.$$

$$\widehat{\mu}_{\beta,t}=\text{point-in-time predicted slope for the new period beginning at date }t.$$

$$\widehat{\mu}_{i,t}=\text{stock-level expected active return at date }t.$$

Delete the separate Relationship to shrunk historical slopes and STOCK LEVEL EXPECTED ACTIVE RETURNS sections because the relevant material is contained here without introducing additional aliases.

OVERLAPPING LABELS, UNIQUENESS, AND DEPENDENCE-AWARE ROBUSTNESS

RETURN-LABEL LEAKAGE

At forecast date $$t$$, only completed historical outcomes may be used:

$$W_h(t)=\left\{s:e_h(s)<t\right\}.$$

This rule prevents return-label leakage. It is the expanding-window analogue of purging because historical observations with unresolved labels are excluded.

An additional embargo is not required for the baseline strictly past-to-future expanding window because the estimator never trains on observations occurring after the forecast date. Embargo becomes relevant if the project later uses K-fold or combinatorial cross-validation containing training observations on both sides of a test period.

OVERLAPPING-RETURN DEPENDENCE

Purging unresolved outcomes does not make the completed historical labels independent.

For each historical date $$s$$, define its forward-return label interval:

$$\mathcal{I}_s^{(h)}=\left\{u:s<u\leq e_h(s)\right\}.$$

Let:

$$L_s^{(h)}=\left|\mathcal{I}_s^{(h)}\right|.$$

For each trading date $$u$$, define the number of completed historical labels that include $$u$$:

$$c_u^{(h)}(t)=\sum_{r\in W_h(t)}\mathbf{1}\left\{u\in\mathcal{I}_r^{(h)}\right\}.$$

The average uniqueness of historical date $$s$$ is:

$$\overline{u}_{s,t}^{(h)}=\frac{1}{L_s^{(h)}}\sum_{u\in\mathcal{I}_s^{(h)}}\frac{1}{c_u^{(h)}(t)}.$$

If a return window shares most of its trading days with several other labels, its average uniqueness is low.

If a return window shares few trading days with other labels, its average uniqueness is high.

Define the uniqueness-equivalent count:

$$N_{\mathrm{unique},t}^{(h)}=\sum_{s\in W_h(t)}\overline{u}_{s,t}^{(h)}.$$

This is a diagnostic measure of nonredundant historical information. It should not be described as an exact number of statistically independent observations.

Report through time:

1. The raw number of completed dates, $$|W_h(t)|$$.
2. The uniqueness-equivalent count, $$N_{\mathrm{unique},t}^{(h)}$$.
3. Median average uniqueness.
4. Minimum average uniqueness.
5. The ratio $$N_{\mathrm{unique},t}^{(h)}/|W_h(t)|$$.

PRIMARY ESTIMATOR

The primary empirical-Bayes estimator continues to use every completed historical date with ordinary random-effects precision weights:

$$a_{s,t}=\frac{1}{\widehat{\tau}_{\beta,t}^2+v_s}.$$

This is the main estimator because it is transparent and uses all completed information.

UNIQUENESS-WEIGHTED SENSITIVITY

As an overlap sensitivity, define:

$$a_{s,t}^{\mathrm{uniq}}=\frac{\overline{u}_{s,t}^{(h)}}{\widehat{\tau}_{\beta,t}^2+v_s}.$$

Then:

$$\widehat{\mu}_{\beta,t}^{\mathrm{uniq}}=\frac{\sum_{s\in W_h(t)}a_{s,t}^{\mathrm{uniq}}\widehat{\beta}_s}{\sum_{s\in W_h(t)}a_{s,t}^{\mathrm{uniq}}}.$$

This downweights historical dates whose return windows are highly redundant with neighboring dates.

The uniqueness-weighted estimator is a project sensitivity analysis inspired by López de Prado’$$s$$ sample-uniqueness framework. It is not presented as a standard closed-form empirical-Bayes result.

Use the same baseline estimate $$\widehat{\tau}_{\beta,t}^2$$ in the ordinary and uniqueness-weighted means. Do not introduce a separate uniqueness-weighted between-date variance unless the primary results are highly sensitive.

DEPENDENCE-PRESERVING BOOTSTRAP

For date-level empirical-Bayes uncertainty, resample the paired historical records:

$$\left(\widehat{\beta}_s,v_s\right).$$

The coefficient and its sampling variance must remain paired.

For each bootstrap replicate:

1. Resample contiguous blocks of historical dates.
2. Reconstruct the bootstrap sequence of paired date-level records.
3. Recompute $$\widehat{\tau}_{\beta,t}^2$$.
4. Recompute $$\widehat{\mu}_{\beta,t}$$.
5. Recompute $$\widehat{\mu}_{\beta,t}^{+}$$.
6. Recompute the uniqueness-weighted sensitivity when required.
7. Save the resulting estimator.

Do not state that the historical cross-sectional slopes are re-estimated from individual stocks unless the bootstrap actually resamples complete stock-date cross-sections.

For the 63-day horizon, use a moving-block bootstrap with block length three monthly rebalance observations as the primary specification.

Report block lengths two and four as sensitivities.

A stationary bootstrap with expected block length three may be reported as an additional sensitivity because it randomizes block lengths and reduces fixed-boundary artifacts.

For the 21-day horizon, use Newey-West uncertainty with at least one lag and report a short block-bootstrap sensitivity.

Use at least 5,000 bootstrap replicates for final reported confidence intervals.

NON-OVERLAPPING 63-DAY ESTIMATES

Construct three staggered 63-day sequences:

Sequence A: $$s_1,s_4,s_7,...$$

Sequence B: $$s_2,s_5,s_8,...$$

Sequence C: $$s_3,s_6,s_9,...$$

Do not assume that this index construction guarantees non-overlap. Verify the actual trading-date intervals.

Within a valid sequence, require:

$$\mathcal{I}_{s_j}^{(63)}\cap\mathcal{I}_{s_k}^{(63)}=\varnothing\quad\text{for }j\neq k.$$

For the all-date estimator and each valid non-overlapping sequence, compare:

1. Estimated population mean.
2. Estimated between-date variance.
3. Current unrestricted forecast slope.
4. Sign-constrained forecast slope.
5. Variance-floor frequency.
6. Zero-floor frequency.

The non-overlapping estimates are robustness checks. They are not expected to be more precise because they use fewer dates.

WHAT EACH METHOD DOES

Completed-outcome filtering addresses future-label leakage.

Concurrency and uniqueness quantify redundancy among completed labels.

Newey-West inference estimates uncertainty in time-series summary statistics.

Block bootstrap preserves short-range dependence when constructing confidence intervals.

Non-overlapping sequences test whether conclusions survive after removing mechanical return-window overlap.

None of these methods eliminates factor-selection bias, LLM pretraining contamination, sector exposure, or universe survivorship.

The fixed-effect benchmark is:

$$\widehat{\mu}_{\beta,t}^{FE}=\frac{\sum_{s\in W_h(t)}v_s^{-1}\widehat{\beta}_s}{\sum_{s\in W_h(t)}v_s^{-1}}.$$

The random-effects empirical-Bayes estimator is:

$$\widehat{\mu}_{\beta,t}=\frac{\sum_{s\in W_h(t)}\left(\widehat{\tau}_{\beta,t}^2+v_s\right)^{-1}\widehat{\beta}_s}{\sum_{s\in W_h(t)}\left(\widehat{\tau}_{\beta,t}^2+v_s\right)^{-1}}.$$

The raw standardized signal should not be included in the slope-calibration comparison because it is unitless and is not a slope estimate. It remains an important portfolio benchmark.

Retain empirical Bayes only if it improves forecast error, stability, or portfolio behavior relative to the simpler zero, unweighted-mean, and fixed-effect benchmarks.

REPLACE: CURRENT Q5 FORECAST
============================================================

CURRENT Q5 FORECAST

Let $$\widehat{\mu}_{\delta,t}$$ denote the empirical-Bayes estimate of the population mean Q5-versus-non-Q5 premium using only completed historical dates.

The optional nonnegative premium is:

$$\widehat{\mu}_{\delta,t}^{+}=\max\left\{0,\widehat{\mu}_{\delta,t}\right\}.$$

Let:

$$I_{i,t}=\mathbf{1}\left\{i\in Q5_t\right\}.$$

Let:

$$p_t=\frac{1}{N_t}\sum_i I_{i,t}.$$

The centered Q5 stock-level forecast is:

$$\widehat{\mu}_{i,t,Q5}=\widehat{\mu}_{\delta,t}^{+}\left(I_{i,t}-p_t\right).$$

This guarantees:

$$\sum_i\widehat{\mu}_{i,t,Q5}=0.$$

The Q5 estimator is not another scaling of the continuous signal. It creates a two-level forecast structure and should be treated as a distinct nonlinear estimator.

Do not introduce an additional symbol such as $$\widehat{d}_t$$ for $$\widehat{\mu}_{\delta,t}$$. Use the population-mean notation directly.
